01

The Road to Five Stars — FoodHub

Python FoundationsA · 4.00February 2024

Exploratory analysis of 1,898 food-delivery orders across cuisine, cost, delivery time, ratings and repeat behavior.

Problem

FoodHub, a food-delivery aggregator, holds order, delivery-time and rating data but no clear view of what drives customer satisfaction or why nearly four in ten orders are never rated.

Objective

Explore the order data to identify demand patterns, delivery bottlenecks and gaps in customer feedback, and turn them into operational recommendations.

38.8%of orders carry no rating
+5.9 minweekday delivery over weekend
65%of customers ordered once

Approach

Data
1,898 orders, 9 variables; rating stored as text with 736 'Not given'
Tools
Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn
Analysis
Univariate and multivariate; total time binned fast / moderate / slow
Imputation
Missing ratings filled from cuisine × day × time-bin group means

Outcome

Nine recommendations for the platform

  • Two-part rating prompt (food, delivery) with reason checkboxes
  • Delivery confirmation as the trigger for an automated rating request
  • Proactive outreach and compensation on slow orders
  • Collect order-ready time, geography, timestamps and driver IDs to diagnose the weekday lag
  • Loyalty program aimed at the 65% who ordered once
  • Exclusive offers with the four promotion-eligible restaurants

Airbnb Pricing Improvement

Machine LearningA+ · 4.33March 2024

Linear and polynomial regression predicting log listing price from 74,111 Airbnb records; a pricing strategy read from the coefficients.

Problem

Hosts set prices by intuition. Airbnb wants pricing to rest on evidence: which attributes of a listing move the price, and by how much.

Objective

Build a regression model that predicts listing price from property attributes, judged on RMSE, MAE and R² on a held-out 30% test set, and read a pricing strategy from its coefficients.

0.524test R², polynomial with interactions
−1.04log-price effect of a shared room vs entire home
6 of 11features retained by forward selection at almost no cost

Approach

Data
74,111 records → 54,117 after dropping duplicates; median imputation by room type; outliers floored and capped
Models
Linear regression; polynomial regression, interaction terms only; linear on forward-selected features
Selection
Sequential forward selection, 5-fold CV (mlxtend)
Split
70 / 30 train–test
Metrics
RMSE, MAE, R², adjusted R², MAPE

Outcome

Polynomial regression selected — best on every metric, no train–test gap

  • Lean on entire-home listings; they command the highest prices
  • Invest in bathrooms and bedrooms — the strongest positive coefficients
  • Test disabling instant booking and folding the cleaning fee into the headline price
  • Segment shared rooms for budget travelers
  • Collect location, amenities and seasonality — the drivers the data lacks

Enhancing Customer Retention Through Predictive Analytics

Advanced Machine LearningA+ · 4.33March 2024

Boosting ensembles tuned across original, undersampled and SMOTE-oversampled data to flag credit-card customers at risk of attrition.

Problem

A bank is losing credit-card customers and finds out only after they have left. It wants to know what behaviors signal which clients are about to leave.

Objective

Classify customers by attrition risk with recall as the primary metric — a missed attriter costs more than an unnecessary retention offer — while keeping precision acceptable.

0.975test recall — AdaBoost, undersampled
0.941test accuracy
0.27feature importance of total transaction amount, the top predictor

Approach

Data
10,127 customers, 16% attrited; most-frequent imputation; one-hot encoding
Models
AdaBoost, gradient boosting, XGBoost, random forest
Tuning
Randomized search; pipelines
Split
80 / 15 / 5 train–test–validation
Imbalance
Original, random undersampling, SMOTE — each model trained on all three
Dropped
Binned-feature variants of every model underperformed

Outcome

AdaBoost on undersampled data — recall 0.975 at precision 0.739, trained on real records only

  • Monitor quarter-on-quarter transaction change
  • Tie rewards to transaction volume
  • Cross-sell to widen each relationship
  • Run the model on the live book
  • Equip frontline staff with the risk score and its drivers

04

Banking on AI for Customer Retention

Introduction to Neural NetworksA · 4.00April 2024

Fifteen feed-forward network configurations in Keras — optimizers, dropout, class weights, SMOTE, regularization and thresholding — to identify bank customers who will leave.

Problem

About one in five customers has stopped using the bank's services. Retention effort is spread evenly because nobody knows who is at risk.

Objective

Train a neural network that identifies customers who will exit, optimized for recall on the exit class, and give the business the levers — threshold, features.

15configurations compared on validation recall
0.93test recall, selected model
49%exit rate in the 45–55 age band

Approach

Data
10,000 customers, 20% exited; identifiers removed; quantile-binned age and balance
Framework
TensorFlow / Keras sequential networks
Varied
SGD vs Adam; dropout; He initialization; batch normalization; class weights vs SMOTE; L1 / L2; tanh vs ReLU; decision threshold 0.5 → 0.3
Metric
Recall on the exit class; precision, F1 and accuracy reported alongside

Outcome

128–64–32 ReLU network, dropout, batch norm, L2, class weights, threshold 0.3 — recall 0.93 at precision 0.26

  • The threshold is the dial: set it with the business against the cost of a false alarm
  • Target retention in Germany; learn from Spain
  • Re-engage inactive members; tailor offers to women and the 45–55 band
  • Collect transaction, utilization and complaint data — for a more complete view

AgroScope — Plant Seedling Classifier

Introduction to Computer VisionA+ · 4.33May 2024

A convolutional network classifying 4,750 seedling images into 12 species, developed through eight architectures from a 71% baseline to a 92% regularized, class-weighted model.

Problem

Agriculture still relies on people walking fields to tell seedlings from weeds. Identification is slow, inconsistent and does not scale.

Objective

Build a convolutional network that classifies a seedling image into one of twelve species accurately enough to automate field monitoring, judged on test accuracy and per-class recall.

92%test accuracy, final CNN
71%baseline accuracy
0.50recall on Black-grass, the one remaining weak class

Approach

Data
4,750 images, 12 species, 128 × 128 → 64 × 64; train 3,847 · validation 428 · test 475
Architecture
Four conv blocks with L2 and batch norm → dense 64 with dropout → softmax
Training
Flip and rotation augmentation; balanced class weights; ReduceLROnPlateau; Adam
Rejected
VGG16 and ResNet50 with frozen bases collapsed to the majority class (14%)

Outcome

Final CNN 92% test accuracy against a 71% baseline; macro F1 0.91

  • Mobile field app and integration with farm-management systems
  • Automated retraining pipeline as new images arrive
  • Targeted data and augmentation for Black-grass vs Loose Silky-bent
  • Fine-tuned transfer learning at native resolution as the next architecture

Automated Support-Ticket Triage with a Local LLM

Introduction to NLP · CapstoneA+ · 4.33June 2024

A four-stage prompting pipeline on Mistral-7B-Instruct, run locally with llama.cpp, that categorizes, tags, prioritizes and drafts replies to support tickets as structured JSON.

Problem

Support tickets arrive as free text. Someone must read each one, decide what it is about and how urgent, set an expectation and write a first reply — slow, inconsistent and expensive at volume.

Objective

Use a locally hosted language model to perform all four triage steps automatically and return structured fields a ticketing system can consume, with every output valid JSON.

4chained prompting tasks
21tickets triaged end to end, all parseable
7Bparameter open model, no data leaving the machine

Approach

Model
Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.2, GGUF Q6_K, via llama-cpp-python; temperature 0.01
Prompts
One per task with an explicit JSON schema, guideline rules and few-shot examples
Chaining
Category → tags → priority and ETA → draft reply, each stage conditioned on the last
Parsing
Responses sliced from the first brace, loaded as JSON, normalized into one table

Outcome

21 tickets triaged end to end, every response parseable

  • Staff hardware and data-recovery support to the queue's shape
  • Add a triage step inside the High tier — 15 of 21 landed there
  • Standardize ETAs within category
  • Mine tags for recurring problems
  • Limits stated: no ground truth, priority skew, category drift, batch-only latency
